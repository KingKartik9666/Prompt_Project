A **Bell state** is a specific quantum state of two qubits that represents the maximum possible degree of entanglement. Named after physicist John Stewart Bell, these states are the foundation of quantum computing and quantum information theory (used in teleportation and superdense coding).

There are four possible Bell states, known collectively as the Bell basis:

1. $|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$
2. $|\Phi^-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle - |11\rangle)$
3. $|\Psi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle + |10\rangle)$
4. $|\Psi^-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle - |10\rangle)$

To create the most common Bell state ($|\Phi^+\rangle$) in a quantum computer, you only need two standard quantum logic gates: **a Hadamard (H) gate** and a **Controlled-NOT (CNOT) gate**.

Here is the step-by-step process of how it works mathematically and physically.

---

### Step-by-Step Guide to Creating $|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$

#### Step 1: Initialize the Qubits
Start with two qubits, both set to the classical state of zero ($|0\rangle$). 
* Mathematically, the combined system is written as $|00\rangle$ (meaning Qubit A is 0, and Qubit B is 0).

#### Step 2: Apply a Hadamard Gate to the First Qubit
Pass the first qubit (Qubit A) through a **Hadamard gate ($H$)**. The Hadamard gate puts a qubit into a state of superposition—giving it an equal probability of being measured as 0 or 1.
* The state of Qubit A becomes $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)$.
* Because Qubit B is untouched, the combined state of the two qubits is now:
  $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle) \otimes |0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |10\rangle)$$

#### Step 3: Apply a CNOT Gate
Next, apply a **Controlled-NOT (CNOT) gate**, using Qubit A as the *control* qubit and Qubit B as the *target* qubit. 
* The CNOT gate looks at the control qubit. If the control is $1$, it flips the target qubit. If the control is $0$, it does nothing.
  * In the first half of our superposition, Qubit A is $0$, so Qubit B remains $0$ ($|00\rangle$).
  * In the second half of our superposition, Qubit A is $1$, so Qubit B is flipped from $0$ to $1$ ($|11\rangle$).

The resulting state is:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$$

**Congratulations, you have created a Bell state!** 

---

### Why is this state special (Entanglement)?
If you measure Qubit A, you have a 50% chance of getting 0 and a 50% chance of getting 1. However, *because they are entangled*, the moment you measure Qubit A and find out what it is, Qubit B is instantly determined:
* If you measure Qubit A and get **0**, Qubit B will **always** be **0**.
* If you measure Qubit A and get **1**, Qubit B will **always** be **1**.

---

### How to create the other 3 Bell states:
By adding simple Pauli gates (X or Z gates) before or after the circuit described above, you can create all four Bell states:

* **$|\Phi^+\rangle$**: $H$ on Qubit A, then CNOT.
* **$|\Phi^-\rangle$**: $H$ on Qubit A, then CNOT, then apply a **Z gate** to Qubit A (or B).
* **$|\Psi^+\rangle$**: $H$ on Qubit A, then CNOT, then apply an **X gate** (NOT gate) to Qubit B.
* **$|\Psi^-\rangle$**: $H$ on Qubit A, then CNOT, then apply a **Z gate** to Qubit A *and* an **X gate** to Qubit B.
